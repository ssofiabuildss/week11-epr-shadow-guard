## 0:30–1:15 — First prohibited claim
[Click RC-101, point to red blocked status and reason.]
"Here the producer has documentation, but we don't have independent downstream acceptance evidence. The system blocks the recovery claim and says exactly why. It doesn't fill missing evidence with an AI guess."

## 1:15–1:50 — Double-counting
[Click RC-102.]
"This record reuses a recovery identifier. That may mean double counting, so the tool flags the risk instead of treating the uploaded file as proof. The system can identify a conflict, but it cannot establish the physical outcome on its own."

## 1:50–2:20 — Informal collector / worker exclusion
[Click RC-103.]
"Now this is the Shadow Clause that matters to me. A fictional informal collector has a paper receipt. The recovery claim stays blocked because downstream proof is missing, but the paper receipt is still eligible for human review. The person isn't rejected for lacking a digital record."

## 2:20–3:00 — Dragon Stack and controls
[Scroll to right-side Dragon Stack; point to each label; scroll to Condition 6.]
"For the required Dragon Stack I added a clearly simulated IoT scale feed, an ML anomaly indicator trained on fictional weights, and geodata distances using fictional facilities. None of them can verify recycling. The claim rules and human-review gate remain separate. The demo doesn't collect worker profiles or real personal data."

## 3:00–3:30 — What changed my mind
[Return to main decision area; camera or voice only according to professor's recording expectations.]
"At first I thought stronger documentation would make compliance more trustworthy. But the build made the limitation obvious: a record can be accurate as a record and still fail to prove that plastic was recycled. I also realized a strict digital rule could exclude the people actually collecting materials. I kept the blocked claims, but made the paper-review route visible. A working prototype doesn't prove market demand, worker safety, or environmental impact."
